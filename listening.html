Listening

The same test for Academic and General. The audio plays once — so this module trains your ear and your test habits. Work one card at a time.

Rough targets: ~30/40 → Band 7 · ~35/40 → Band 8. You don't need perfection — you need to stop the leaks. That's what the Error Log is for.

Four sections, 40 questions, ~30 minutes of audio that plays once. Difficulty rises as you go.

You didn't catch answer 12. What should you do?

The audio never repeats. Freezing on a missed answer makes you miss the next 2–3 as well (the "cascade"). Drop it and move on — you can guess at the end.

Foundation drill (5 min): play any clip above and shadow it — repeat aloud 1–2 seconds behind the speaker, copying their rhythm and stress. This tunes your ear faster than passive listening.

You lose easy marks to mechanics, not comprehension. Burn these in:

Spelling counts. "Wendsday" = 0. British spelling is standard; American spellings are accepted.
Plurals matter. "book" ≠ "books". Listen for the final /s/.
Write digits. "2:30", not "two thirty" — faster and safer.
The word limit is absolute. "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" → three words scores 0, even if correct.
Default to CAPITALS. Writing everything in CAPS removes capitalisation guesswork and is always accepted.

Instruction: "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER." The answer you hear is "a wooden desk". What do you write?

"a wooden desk" is three words → 0 marks. "wooden desk" is two words and keeps the meaning. Articles like "a/the" usually can be dropped to stay inside the limit.

One habit beats them all: use the short gap before each section to pre-read the questions and underline keywords. Knowing what's coming turns listening into confirming.

One 5-minute academic lecture, 10 questions, no break to re-read. This is where most people drop 4+ marks.

Pre-read all 10 questions in the gap before it starts. Group them by subtopic.
Use the lecture's signposts as anchors: "Today I'll cover…", "Moving on to…", "Finally…".
Listen for the slow-down. Lecturers stress and slow down right on the answer. Train your ear to catch that.
Pre-learn topic vocabulary (research methods, history, biology, environment). Familiar words are easier to catch at speed — build them in the Lexis Bank.

Rotate these — 10–15 minutes each. They train different muscles.

Shadowing → rhythm & sound. Repeat a 60-sec clip 1–2 sec behind the speaker.
Dictation → Section 1 accuracy. Play a 30-sec address/phone/date clip, write it, check spelling, log errors.
Prediction → Section 4. Before audio, predict each gap's word class (noun? number? verb?).
Speed-spell → the cheapest band-saver. Have a partner or text-to-speech spell 20 names + 10 phone numbers under time pressure.

A repeatable 5-step loop for one practice section:

1. Pick one section (Cambridge IELTS 17–19 is best, or British Council free tests).
2. Pre-read the questions (30 sec). Underline keywords, predict word class.
3. Play once at normal speed. Don't pause. Write on the booklet.
4. Mark it. Log every wrong answer in the Error Log with a reason (misheard / spelling / wrong word class / distractor / out-of-order).
5. Replay with the transcript. Find the exact word you missed and the cue before it. Re-listen to that 10-second chunk 3×.
Band-droppers: freezing on a missed answer · reading ahead while audio still covers the current question · going over the word limit · training only one accent.
Do this now: start a 25-min focus block and run the 5-step loop on one Section 1. Log at least one error.
