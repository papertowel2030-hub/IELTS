Writing

Two tasks in 60 minutes. Task 1 (~20 min, 150+ words) is a lettera report on a visual; Task 2 (~40 min, 250+ words) is an essay worth double the marks. Toggle your test type in the sidebar — Task 1 below adapts.

This is where most people stall. The cause is almost always the same: rigid memorised templates that cap you at Band 6. This module teaches you to write to the four criteria instead.

Your Writing band is the average of four criteria, each worth 25%. Know what each one rewards:

CriterionQuestion it asksWhat Band 7 needs
Task ResponseDid you answer it?Clear position throughout · all parts covered · ideas extended, not just stated
Coherence & CohesionIs it organised?Logical paragraphs · one clear topic each · cohesion that doesn't call attention to itself
Lexical ResourceVocabulary?Precise, topic-specific words · correct collocations · a few less-common items that fit
GrammarSentence range?A variety of complex structures · the majority of sentences error-free
Read the official rubric once on the Band Descriptors page. You're aiming at a defined target — see the target.

Three "tips" from the internet actively hold you at Band 6. Unlearn them:

Myth: spray linking words (However, Moreover, Furthermore, In addition…). Examiners are trained to spot the over-use list. Band-7 cohesion "attracts no attention" — over-use pulls you down.
Myth: insert "complex" sentences with because/although/which. Mechanically bolting on subordinators isn't range. Real range = relative clauses, conditionals, passives, participle clauses — used where they fit, not as a quota.
Myth: memorise fancy words (plethora, myriad, ubiquitous). If they don't fit the topic precisely, they're marked as inappropriate lexis. Band 7 rewards collocation accuracy, not impressive-sounding words.

First, read the prompt and lock the tone — it decides everything else:

Formal — unknown/official recipient (a manager, the council). "Dear Sir or Madam," → "Yours faithfully,"
Semi-formal — known by name/professionally (your named landlord). "Dear Mr Patel," → "Yours sincerely,"
Informal — a friend or family member. "Dear Sam," → "Best wishes,"

Then cover all three bullet points. Structure serves clarity — not a rigid rule:

Opening — state your purpose in one sentence. Body — cover all 3 bullets (one per paragraph, or grouped 2+1). Put the "request" bullet last. Closing — the matching sign-off.
FunctionFormalInformal
OpenI am writing to express my concern regarding…I'm writing to let you know about…
RequestI would be grateful if you could…Could you sort out…?
CloseYours faithfully,Best wishes,
Instant band-drop: ignoring one of the three bullets. Check them off before you write the sign-off. Annotated letters: Model Answers.

You describe a graph, chart, table, map or process in 150+ words. No opinion, no guessing why — only what the visual shows.

Intro — one sentence, paraphrasing what the visual shows (don't copy the caption).
Overview — the 2 biggest trends, with no specific numbers. This is mandatory and worth roughly a quarter of your Task 1 mark.
2 detail paragraphs — group the data logically and quote selected figures to support the trends.

Language to drill:

PurposePhrases
Trendsrose, surged, dipped, plateaued, fluctuated, tripled
Comparisonhigher than, twice as many as, similar to, the gap narrowed
Process (passive)the materials are collected, then they are sorted…
Instant band-drop: adding opinion ("this is worrying") or skipping the overview. Annotated report: Model Answers.

Spend your first 60 seconds identifying the essay type — each one wants a different job done:

TypePrompt cueWhat to deliver
Opinion"To what extent…?", "Do you agree?"One clear position, defended all the way through
Discussion"Discuss both views and give your opinion."Fair coverage of both sides plus your stance — don't hide it
Problem / Solution"What problems…? What solutions…?"Both halves developed — one line of solutions loses marks
Advantages / Disadvantages"…advantages and disadvantages."Balanced (or weighted if it asks "do they outweigh")
Two-part"Why…? What can be done?"Answer both questions explicitly

"Some people think exams are the best way to assess students. Discuss both views and give your own opinion." Which structure fits?

"Discuss both views and give your own opinion" is the discussion type. If you only argue one side, you miss half the task → Task Response capped low.

Spend 5 of your 40 minutes planning. It feels slow; it saves the essay.

Decode (30s): underline the keywords and the instruction word.
Position (60s): decide your stance — strong, partial, or balanced, but explicit.
Two ideas (2 min): one per body paragraph, distinct and defensible.
Examples (90s): everyday examples are fine. Never invent fake statistics — examiners are trained to ignore "a 2019 study showed 73%…".

Then pick the structure that matches the type:

Opinion → Intro (paraphrase + position) · Reason 1 (explain+example) · Reason 2 · Conclusion Discussion → Intro (both views + your lean) · View A · View B (+ where you stand) · Conclusion Problem/Solution → Intro · Problems · Matched solutions · Conclusion Two-part → Intro · Answer Q1 · Answer Q2 · Conclusion

Coherence: one clear topic sentence per paragraph (the reader knows the point in 10 words). Connect ideas with reference words (this, such, the latter) and structure — not a connector in every sentence. If you used 5+ linkers, delete half.

Lexical Resource: use topic-specific words and correct collocations:

make a decision ✓ / do a decision ✗  ·  heavy traffic ✓ / strong traffic ✗  ·  ease congestion, mitigate the impact ✓ (when they fit). Build these in the Lexis Bank.

Grammar range — mix sentence types; deploy these where they fit:

Relative clause: "Children who grow up in cities tend to…" · Conditional: "If governments had invested earlier, we wouldn't…" · Participle: "Faced with rising costs, families…" · Passive: "Renewables must be subsidised."
Accuracy beats complexity. The Band-7 grammar bar is "the majority of sentences are error-free". A simple correct sentence outscores a fancy broken one.
Length: aim ~280–310 words for Task 2, ~170–200 for Task 1. Too short = automatic penalty. Too long = accuracy drops and you steal checking time.

Weekly rhythm:

Weekdays: hand-write one body paragraph (or a letter/report intro). Self-score against the descriptors. Add 3 collocations to Flashcards.
Weekend: full Task 1 + Task 2, timed (60 min), by hand. Then self-mark, compare to a model, and rewrite your weakest paragraph.
Fortnightly: get one essay marked by someone who can grade to Band 7 — see Feedback. You can't self-grade past Band 6.
Band-droppers: memorised openings ("In this modern era…") · a connector every sentence · a generic essay forced onto the specific question · ignoring a bullet/part · one giant paragraph · fake statistics · copying the question wording instead of paraphrasing.
Do this now: start a 25-min block, write one Task 2 body paragraph on any prompt, then log its weakest criterion in the Error Log.
