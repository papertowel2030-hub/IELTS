Task 2 · Opinion essay ~Band 7.5

Some people believe governments should invest more in public transport rather than building new roads. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Intro — As congestion and emissions worsen in many cities, the question of whether public funds are best spent on expanding public transport or constructing additional roads has become increasingly pressing. I largely agree that investment should favour public transport, although a measured commitment to road maintenance remains necessary.

TR: paraphrases the prompt, clear position with nuance. LR: "pressing", "measured commitment". GRA: complex sentence + adverbial opening.

Body 1 — The strongest argument for prioritising public transport is its capacity to ease congestion at scale. A single light-rail line can carry the equivalent of several lanes of traffic, which means each pound spent on transit yields a far greater reduction in commute times than the same sum poured into widening a motorway. London's Elizabeth line, for example, has shifted millions of daily journeys off the road. Building more roads, conversely, often triggers induced demand: new lanes fill with new cars within a few years.

TR: idea extended with reasoning + example + counter-mechanism. CC: clear topic sentence; one connector only. LR: "ease congestion", "induced demand". GRA: relative clause, passive ("has shifted").

Body 2 — Beyond traffic, transit investment delivers environmental and social dividends that roads cannot match. Trains emit far less CO₂ per passenger than private cars, helping cities meet climate targets. Equally important is equity: those who cannot afford a car depend on transit to reach work and services. That said, some road spending is still warranted for freight and emergency access; the choice is one of emphasis, not exclusion.

TR: second idea (environment + equity) with a concession — nuance lifts TR. LR: "dividends". GRA: cleft ("the choice is one of"), participle clause ("helping cities meet…").

Conclusion — On balance, governments should direct the greater share of transport budgets to public systems, since these deliver larger gains in congestion, emissions and equity than equivalent road spending. Roads will still matter — but as the supporting cast in a transit-led strategy.

TR: restates position with reasons. LR: precise metaphor. GRA: subordinate clause + comparison.

Task 2 · Discussion essay ~Band 7

Some think young people should follow their society's traditions. Others think they should be free to behave as individuals. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Intro — Whether young adults ought to honour inherited customs or chart an independent course is a long-standing tension. Both positions have merit, but I lean towards the view that individuality, exercised with respect for tradition, produces the healthiest outcome.

TR: previews both sides + signals stance. LR: "chart an independent course".

Body 1 — Defenders of tradition argue that customs give young people identity, continuity and a tested moral framework. Rituals and etiquette transmit values that have endured because they work — they bind communities and reduce the rootlessness that often accompanies rapid change.

TR: fair coverage of view A with reasoning. LR: "tested moral framework", "rootlessness". GRA: present perfect.

Body 2 — On the other hand, uncritical adherence to tradition can entrench inequality and stifle innovation. Many rights now taken for granted were won precisely because young people refused to follow the script. My own view aligns here: a society that cannot tolerate dissent from its norms will eventually pay for it in talent and progress.

TR: view B + own opinion embedded (acceptable). LR: "entrench inequality", "stifle innovation". GRA: cleft ("were won precisely because").

Conclusion — In short, traditions deserve thoughtful engagement, not blind allegiance. Young people flourish when they draw on inherited wisdom while reserving the right to revise it — and societies that allow this are the ones that endure.

TR: restates balance + stance. LR: "blind allegiance", "inherited wisdom".

Task 1 · Formal letter ~Band 7+

You bought a new smartphone online but it arrived damaged. Write to the company: explain the situation; describe the damage; say what you want them to do.

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to formally complain about a smartphone I purchased from your online store on 12 May, which arrived in a damaged condition.

Opening: purpose in one sentence, formal register, includes the date.

The order, reference A-44219, was delivered last Friday. When I opened the package, the screen had a long crack across its lower half and the rear casing was visibly dented. The box itself appeared intact, which suggests the damage occurred before dispatch. I have kept the packaging and taken photographs, which I can provide on request.

Bullets 1–2: specific detail = TR fulfilled. LR: "visibly dented", "intact". GRA: relative clause ("which suggests").

Given that the device is unusable and the fault is clearly not mine, I would be grateful if you could arrange a full replacement of the same model at no additional cost. If that is not feasible within seven working days, I would prefer a complete refund to the original payment method.

Bullet 3: clear request + alternative. GRA: conditional, hedged tone ("would be grateful").

I look forward to your prompt response.

Yours faithfully,
[Your Name]

Closing: formal sign-off matching "Dear Sir or Madam".

Task 1 · Informal letter ~Band 7+

A friend is visiting next month. Tell them: when to come; what to bring; what you'll do together.

Hi Maya,

I was so happy to hear you're finally coming over next month — I've been looking forward to this for ages.

If you can, try to arrive in the second half of June. The weather will be warmer and the rains usually hold off until July, so we'll get the best of the long evenings. I'll have a few days off around the 20th, which is when I'd love to show you around properly.

As for packing: light clothes mostly, good walking shoes, and a thin jacket for the evenings — it cools down more than you'd expect. Don't bother bringing adapters, I've got spares.

I've got a few things lined up: a day in the old town, sunrise at the hill temple (worth the early start, trust me), and the night market — the street food alone is a reason to visit. If you're up for it, we can drive out to the coast too.

Let me know your flight details once you've booked and I'll come pick you up. Can't wait!
Sam

Informal tone: contractions, phrasal verbs ("hold off", "pick up"), idiom ("worth the early start"). All 3 bullets covered; cohesion via reference, not connector-spam.

Task 1 · Bar-chart report ~Band 7+

The chart shows the % of households in three regions (A, B, C) with broadband access in 2005, 2015 and 2024. Summarise the main features and make comparisons.

The bar chart compares broadband internet penetration across three regions — A, B and C — at three points in time: 2005, 2015 and 2024.

Intro: paraphrases the prompt, no copying.

Overall, broadband access expanded substantially in all three regions over the two decades, but the pace differed sharply: Region A led throughout, Region C closed most of the gap, while Region B remained the slowest to adopt.

Overview: the 2 big trends, no specific figures. Mandatory — and often missed.

In 2005, only about 18% of households in Region A had broadband, compared with 9% in B and just 3% in C. By 2015, Region A had more than tripled to roughly 62%, while B reached 41% and C climbed to 24%. The largest leap came between 2015 and 2024 for Region C, which surged to around 78%, narrowing the gap with Region A (91%) to about ten percentage points. Region B grew more modestly, ending at 70%.

Detail: selected figures + comparison language ("tripled", "surged", "narrowing the gap"). GRA: past simple + past perfect, comparatives.

By the end of the period, broadband had become near-universal in Region A and was approaching that status in C, whereas B — despite its early head start over C — fell behind in relative terms.

Closing comparison sharpens the trend. No opinion, no speculation.
