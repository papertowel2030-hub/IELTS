Writing — Target Band 7.0+

2 tasks in 60 min. T1: letter (GT) / report (Academic), 20 min, 150+ words. T2: essay, 40 min, 250+ words. T2 = double the marks. Write by hand if paper test booked.
Most important page on this site. Writing is where most CLB 9/10 candidates fail. Reason: they follow rigid templates that cap at Band 6. To pass Band 7 you must understand the four descriptors and write to them, not to a template.

Why rigid templates cap you at Band 6

Myth: "5+ linking words: however, therefore, in addition, on the other hand, consequently…" Examiners are trained to spot the over-use list. CC band 7 says "uses cohesion in such a way that it attracts no attention." Over-use = Band 6.
Myth: "3+ complex sentences with because/although/which/if." Mechanical insertion of subordinators ≠ Grammatical Range. Range means: relative clauses, conditionals, passive voice, participle clauses, cleft structures, inversion. Used where they fit, not as a quota.
Myth: "Memorise advanced vocabulary like 'plethora', 'myriad', 'ubiquitous'." If they don't fit the topic precisely, examiners mark them as inappropriate lexis. LR Band 7 wants collocation accuracy, not impressive-sounding words.

Task 1 (GT) — Letter

Tone (must match the prompt)

  • Formal — official, unknown recipient (manager, council, company). "Dear Sir/Madam," → "Yours faithfully,"
  • Semi-formal — known professionally (your boss, your landlord by name). "Dear Mr Patel," → "Yours sincerely,"
  • Informal — friend, family. "Dear Sam," → "Best wishes,"

Flexible structure (NOT a rigid 3-paragraph rule)

Opening — state purpose in 1 sentence. Body — cover all 3 bullet points. Paragraphing serves clarity: • 3 bullets can be 1 paragraph each, or grouped 2+1, depending on flow. • Keep the most actionable bullet (often "request") last. Closing — appropriate sign-off.

Tone calibration: same content, three tones

FunctionFormalSemi-formalInformal

Task 1 (Academic) — Report on a visual

  • Describe a graph / chart / table / map / process. 150+ words.
  • NO opinion, NO speculation. Only what the visual shows.
  • Structure: paraphrased intro · overview (2 big trends, NO numbers) · 2 detail paragraphs with selected figures.
  • Overview is mandatory and worth ~25% of Task Achievement.
  • Language: trends (rise / fall / plateau / fluctuate), comparison (higher than / twice as / similar to), time markers, passive voice for processes.

Task 2 — Essay

Question type recognition (first 60 seconds)

TypePrompt cueWhat examiner wants
Opinion (agree/disagree)"To what extent…", "Do you agree?"Clear position, defended consistently. Partial agreement is allowed if reasoned.
Discussion (both views)"Discuss both views and give your own opinion."Fair coverage of both sides + your stance. Don't bury your view.
Problem / Solution"What problems…? What solutions…?"Both halves must be developed. One sentence on solutions = TR drop.
Advantages / Disadvantages"Discuss the advantages and disadvantages."Balanced if "discuss both"; weighted if "do advantages outweigh".
Two-part question"Why is this? What can be done?"Answer BOTH questions explicitly. Missing one = Band 5 TR.

Planning (use 5 of your 40 min)

Decode (30 sec): Underline the keywords + the question instruction.
Position (60 sec): Decide your stance. Strong, partial, balanced — but explicit.
Two main ideas (2 min): One per body paragraph. Each must be defensible and distinct.
Examples (90 sec): Generic examples count. Don't invent fake statistics ("a 2019 Harvard study showed 73%…" — examiners trained to ignore).

Flexible structures (pick the one that fits the question)

[A] Opinion essay — 4 paragraphs Intro: paraphrase + position Body 1: main reason 1 → explain → example → consequence Body 2: main reason 2 → explain → example → consequence Conclusion: restate position + final implication [B] Discussion essay — 4 paragraphs Intro: paraphrase + preview both views + signal your stance Body 1: View A — explain + example Body 2: View B — explain + example + why your stance leans here Conclusion: restate balance [C] Problem / Solution — 4 paragraphs Intro: paraphrase + signal "problems + solutions" Body 1: 1–2 key problems, explained Body 2: 1–2 matched solutions, explained, realistic Conclusion: short, no new content [D] Two-part — 4 or 5 paragraphs Intro: paraphrase + signal you will answer both Body 1: answer Q1 fully Body 2: answer Q2 fully (Optional Body 3 if Q2 is complex) Conclusion: tie answers together

Coherence done properly (CC Band 7+)

  • One clear topic sentence per body paragraph. Reader knows the paragraph's claim within 10 words.
  • Cohesion via: topic-comment progression · reference (this, such, the latter) · synonyms · ellipsis · punctuation. NOT just connectors.
  • If you used 5+ explicit connectors, replace half with reference or punctuation.

Lexical Resource done properly (LR Band 7+)

  • Topic-specific lexis: environment essay needs "carbon footprint, emissions, sustainability, biodiversity," not "the world is bad."
  • Collocations: make a decision ✓ / do a decision ✗. Heavy traffic ✓ / strong traffic ✗.
  • Less common items used naturally: "exacerbate, mitigate, prevalence, undermine, alleviate" — only when they fit.
  • Build per topic. See lexis bank.

Grammatical Range (GRA Band 7+)

  • Mix: simple, compound, complex, complex-with-multiple-clauses.
  • Structures that signal range when used naturally:
    • Relative clauses: "Children who grow up in cities tend to…"
    • Conditionals (mixed): "If governments had invested earlier, we would not be facing…"
    • Cleft: "What governments must do is invest in renewables."
    • Inversion (sparingly): "Not only does this reduce emissions, but it also creates jobs."
    • Participle clauses: "Faced with rising costs, families have…"
    • Passive (where focus is on the action): "Renewable energy must be subsidised."
  • Accuracy matters more than complexity. Error-free majority of sentences is the GRA Band 7 bar.

Length warning

Aim 280–310 words for T2, 170–200 for T1. Over-long essays hurt: TR (off-topic drift) + GRA (accuracy drops) + time stolen from checking. Under-length = automatic Band penalty.

Annotated model answers

Two annotated Band 7+ essays + two GT letters + one Academic Task 1: model answers page. Every paragraph annotated for which descriptor it earns marks against.

Daily routine (15 min weekday / 90 min weekend)

Weekday: One body paragraph or one letter intro. Hand-write. Self-score against descriptors. Add 3 new collocations to Anki.
Weekend session 1: Full T1 + T2 timed (60 min). Hand-written. No phone.
Weekend session 2: Self-mark using descriptors. Compare to model. Rewrite the weakest paragraph. Submit for external feedback fortnightly (see feedback paths).

Common mistakes that drop your band

  • Memorised opening ("In this modern era of globalisation…") — examiners discount it.
  • Mechanical connectors every sentence.
  • Generic essay applied to specific question (TR drop).
  • One bullet ignored in T1 letter (TR drop).
  • Single-block paragraph (CC drop to Band 5).
  • Bullet points / dashes in the writing itself.
  • No clear position (Opinion / Discussion essays).
  • Fake statistics — examiners ignore them.
  • Repeating the question wording verbatim instead of paraphrasing.
