Before any technique, spend ~10 minutes here. These five cards explain how the test is scored and why this guide works the way it does. One card at a time — tap “Mark done & next”.

This is a training system, not a textbook. You won't read for hours. You'll do short lessons, practise with real material, and track what you get wrong so it stops happening.

The whole thing runs on one simple loop:

Diagnose → Interleave → Retrieve → Reflect. Find your gaps, mix the skills, test yourself, then fix your top mistakes. Repeat weekly.

Speaking is different. You'll learn the full system and techniques here, but you get better at speaking by speaking with a coach — so speaking practice happens in live sessions you book with me.

Myth: "I need to study 6 hours a day."
Truth: 45–60 focused minutes on most days beats long, unfocused marathons. Consistency > intensity.

Four skills, each scored 0–9. Your Overall Band is the average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band.

The rounding rule that catches people: an average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band; .75 rounds up to the next whole band. Example: 6.5 + 6.5 + 6.0 + 7.0 = 26 ÷ 4 = 6.5. But 7 + 6.5 + 6.5 + 6.5 = 26.5 ÷ 4 = 6.625 → 6.5.

You score L7.5, R7.0, W6.5, S6.5. What's your overall band?

7.5 + 7.0 + 6.5 + 6.5 = 27.5 ÷ 4 = 6.875 → rounds up to 7.0. IELTS never reports quarter bands — it's always a whole or half.

These aren't opinions — each is backed by decades of cognitive-science research (numbers link to the sources).

1 · Test yourself, don't re-read. Recalling something is what burns it into memory. Re-reading feels productive but barely helps.1
2 · Space it out. Short daily reps beat cramming. Your flashcards and reviews are spaced on purpose.2
3 · Mix the skills (interleave). Rotating Listening/Reading/Writing/Speaking through the week feels harder but roughly doubles what sticks.3
4 · Log every error. Your mistakes are the syllabus. The Error Log turns each wrong answer into next week's drill.1
If it feels a bit hard, that's the point. Effortful practice ("desirable difficulty") produces stronger memory than easy practice that feels smooth.4

You never have to decide what to do. Here's the daily rhythm:

1. Open the Home dashboard → it shows your one next action.
2. Start a 25-minute focus block and do that lesson or drill.
3. Log anything you got wrong in the Error Log.
4. Do 5 minutes of flashcards. Done for the day. ✅

The full weekly rotation lives in your Study Plan — but you don't need to hold it in your head. The dashboard remembers for you.

Why so structured? Because for ADHD / low-attention brains, the hard part isn't the English — it's starting and keeping track. So the guide keeps the next step visible and small.7

A few things to keep you grounded when motivation dips:

Aim at a target, not "as high as possible." You chose a band for a reason. Hitting it is winning. Don't burn out chasing a 9 you don't need.
Small wins count. One lesson, one logged error, one flashcard round — that's a good day. Your streak counter rewards showing up, not perfection.
Plateaus are normal. Progress in Writing and Speaking is jumpy. Keep logging errors and getting feedback; the jump comes.
