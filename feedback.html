Writing — three ways to get graded

Option A · Most accurate

A certified ex-examiner

A former IELTS examiner marks one essay against the official rubric and returns a band per criterion with comments. Best used at the start, the mid-point, and the final week.

Where: Fiverr (filter "IELTS examiner", check reviews), Writing9's examiner tier, local prep centres, or the r/IELTS community. Verify by asking when they last marked operationally.

Option B · Cheap & fast

An AI grader

Good for rapid weekly iteration on TR/CC/LR/GRA. Use a tool like Writing9, or paste your essay into Claude/ChatGPT with this prompt:

Score this IELTS Task 2 essay against the official band descriptors. Give a band for each criterion (TR, CC, LR, GRA) with one specific reason each, then an overall band, then the single most impactful improvement.
Caveat: AI tends to inflate Band-6 essays to Band 7. Treat the AI band as a ceiling — assume your real band is ~0.5 lower until a human confirms it.
Option C · Free

Calibrated peer review

Swap essays with a study partner — but both read the official descriptors first, or you'll mark each other too kindly. Best as a sanity check on top of A or B. Find partners on r/IELTS or English-conversation meetups.

Always do this first

Self-score against the rubric

  1. Open the band descriptors.
  2. Score your essay/recording on each of the 4 criteria — ruthlessly; your bias is to over-score.
  3. Find your lowest criterion. That's the bottleneck — drill it next week and log it in the Error Log.
  4. Calibrate: if an examiner/AI marks 0.5 lower than you did, subtract that bias from your future self-scores.

Make the feedback count

1. Read the comments the same day; highlight your lowest criterion.
2. Add 3 specific issues to your Error Log.
3. Rewrite the worst paragraph (or re-record the cue card) within 48 hours.
4. Next round, ask: "Last time you flagged X — did I fix it?" People track your progress when prompted.
