Feedback Paths — for Writing & Speaking

You cannot reliably self-grade Writing or Speaking above Band 6. Without external feedback you plateau. This page lists the realistic options, ranked by cost and accuracy.

Why this is the missing link

Listening & Reading are objective — you mark them yourself. Writing & Speaking are subjective, and self-evaluation has a documented ceiling: candidates over-estimate their own band by 0.5–1.0 consistently. The Dunning-Kruger trap. Solution: at least fortnightly external feedback during the 8-week plan.

Option A — Certified ex-examiner (most accurate, highest cost)

  • What: Former IELTS examiner marks one essay or one recorded mock against the official rubric, returns scores per criterion + written comments.
  • Cost: typically £15–35 per task, £25–60 per speaking mock.
  • When to use: diagnostic (Week 1), mid-plan (Week 4), final (Week 7).
  • Where to find:
    • Fiverr — filter for "IELTS examiner". Read reviews; verify credentials.
    • Writing9 — has paid examiner-feedback tier.
    • Local IELTS prep centres often offer single-essay scoring without committing to a course.
    • r/IELTS subreddit — vetted examiners advertise occasionally.
  • Verify: ask for their examiner code or date they last marked operationally. Real examiners can answer this; coaches who claim "examiner experience" often cannot.

Option B — AI grader (cheap, fast, useful with caveats)

  • What: An LLM scores against descriptors. Modern models are reasonable on TR/CC/LR/GRA but less reliable on Pron and on nuanced TR.
  • Cost: free–£10/mo.
  • Where:
    • Writing9 — auto-grader.
    • IELTS Online Tests — auto-grader.
    • ChatGPT / Claude with prompt: "Score this IELTS Task 2 essay against the official descriptors. Give a band per criterion (TR, CC, LR, GRA) with one specific reason per band, then an overall band. Suggest the single most impactful improvement."
  • Caveats: AI tends to inflate Band 6 essays to Band 7. Treat AI grade as ceiling estimate; assume real band 0.5 lower until verified by an examiner.
  • Best use: weekly drafts, rapid iteration. NOT a substitute for one human evaluation per fortnight.

Option C — Peer review with calibrated rubric (free)

  • What: A study partner (same target band) marks your essay using a shared rubric; you mark theirs. Calibration matters — both must read the official descriptors first.
  • Cost: none.
  • Where to find partner:
    • r/IELTS, r/EnglishLearning — partner-finding threads.
    • Local English-conversation meetups.
    • iTalki Community → language partners.
  • Risk: two Band 5 candidates rarely mark each other to Band 7. Peer review works best as a sanity check on top of AI + occasional examiner.

Option D — Conversation partner / tutor for Speaking

  • iTalki — community tutors $5–15/hr; professional tutors $20–40/hr. Filter for "IELTS Speaking preparation" + verified reviews.
  • Preply — similar marketplace.
  • Local language schools — group mock sessions.
  • Frequency: 2× per week of 30-min conversation = realistic fluency gain in 8 weeks.

Option E — Self-rubric (last resort, but always do it FIRST)

  1. Print the band descriptors.
  2. Score your own essay / recording against each of the 4 criteria. Be ruthless — bias is to over-score yourself.
  3. Identify the lowest criterion. That is the bottleneck. Drill it next week.
  4. Submit to Option A or B for calibration: if the human / AI marks 0.5 lower than your self-score, your self-bias is +0.5. Subtract that going forward.

How to act on feedback (don't waste the spend)

  1. Read the comments same day. Highlight the criterion with the lowest score.
  2. Add 3 specific issues from the feedback to your error log.
  3. Rewrite the worst paragraph (Writing) or re-record the same cue card (Speaking) within 48 hours.
  4. At the next feedback round, send a covering note: "Last time you flagged X. Did I fix it?" — examiners track progress when prompted.
