Reading — Target Band 8.0

36 of 40 correct in 60 min. GT: 3 sections, easy → hard. Academic: 3 long passages of equal length. No extra transfer time.
Time budget (GT): S1 ≈ 17 min · S2 ≈ 18 min · S3 ≈ 23 min · transfer + check ≈ 2 min. Hardest section gets the most time, not equal time.

Mindset shift: paraphrase recognition, not skimming

Old advice: "Skim → scan." For Band 8 this is not enough. The real skill is recognising that a question word X has been replaced by a synonym / paraphrase Y in the passage. Train this directly.

Paraphrase drill (do daily)

Take any Cambridge IELTS passage + its question set. For each question, find the question word in the passage. Underline both. After 20 questions you start predicting paraphrase patterns (e.g. "increase" → "rise / growth / upward trend / surge / climb").

True / False / Not Given — decision tree

  1. Find the sentence(s) in the passage that mention the question topic.
  2. Compare the claim in the question to what the passage says.
  3. Ask: "Does the passage support, contradict, or stay silent on this claim?"
    • Support (same meaning, even if paraphrased) → TRUE
    • Contradict (opposite or different value) → FALSE
    • Silent (partial info, no info, related but not the claim) → NOT GIVEN
  4. Warning: if the passage states X but the question says "always X" or "all X" — and the passage said "some" or "often" — that's FALSE, not NG. Quantifier mismatch = contradiction.

Matching Headings — opposite advice to the rest of the test

For headings, "questions first" fails. You need passage gist before reading headings, otherwise the headings prime you toward the wrong fit.

  1. Cover the heading list with your hand.
  2. Skim paragraph A: read first 2 sentences + last sentence. Note its gist in 3–5 words in the margin.
  3. Repeat for B, C, D…
  4. NOW reveal the headings. Match your margin notes to the headings. Eliminate as you go.

Daily routine (15 min weekday / 90 min weekend)

Step 1: One passage from Cambridge IELTS 17–19. Pick a difficulty matching your week (S1 early, S3 later).
Step 2: Look at question types FIRST. Decide order (headings → T/F/NG → completion → matching info).
Step 3: Timer on. Read with purpose — passive reading = wasted time.
Step 4: Mark every wrong answer with category in error log: paraphrase missed / NG vs F confusion / quantifier slip / out-of-context inference / time pressure.
Step 5: Add 5 unknown words to Anki with passage sentence as context.

Common mistakes

  • Treating "Not Given" and "False" as interchangeable.
  • Using outside knowledge (e.g. "I know polar bears are endangered" — passage doesn't say so → NG).
  • Spending 25 min on S1 because it feels safe → no time for S3.
  • Reading passage fully before checking questions (works for one-off study, kills test-day speed).
  • Not transferring as you go.

Academic Reading note

If you booked Academic: passages are 700–900 words each, equal difficulty. Drop the "easy → hard" pacing — split 20-20-20 with 5 min for transfer. Same question types as GT. AWL is more critical.
